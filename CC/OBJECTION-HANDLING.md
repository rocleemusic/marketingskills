# Sales Playbook: Neutralizing Objections (Clean Cuts)

This document is designed for the Clean Cuts leadership team to use during discovery calls to handle the top 3 deal-breakers identified by the CEO.

---

## 1. "We already have a relationship with a studio/freelancer."
**Why they say it:** Status-quo bias. They fear the "friction" of a new workflow more than they desire better sound.
**The Response Approach:** Don't replace; Augment.
**Talk Track:**
> "That’s great—we respect long-standing partnerships. Most of our agency clients actually use us as an **overflow or high-stakes extension** of their team. When you have a project with 'zero margin for error'—like a celebrity narrator or a Super Bowl spot—that's when people pull us in. We plug into your existing workflow via Source Connect, so there’s no transition friction."

## 2. "You're more expensive than our freelancer."
**Why they say it:** They are viewing audio as a commodity, not a creative investment.
**The Response Approach:** Frame as "Insurance against Risk."
**Talk Track:**
> "We certainly aren't the cheapest, and that’s intentional. You aren't just paying for a mix; you're paying for **broadcast-compliance insurance** and Grammy-winning artistry. A cheaper freelancer might save you money upfront, but one technical rejection from HBO or a missed deadline costs significantly more in both money and reputation. We guarantee a 1-hour response time and a mistake-free delivery."

## 3. "We don't know if you have the right experience for THIS specific project."
**Why they say it:** Fear of the unknown. They need specific vertical proof.
**The Response Approach:** Deploy the "Authority Hook."
**Talk Track:**
> "I understand. Every project has its own sonic language. That’s why we were hired for the **Higher Ground (The Obamas)** podcast and **NBA2K**. We’ve likely handled your specific complexity before. Would you like me to send over a 60-second 'Audio Reel' specifically featuring our work in [Prospect's Industry]?"

---

## The "Wedge" Close (The Pivot)
If the deal is stalling, pivot to the "Talent in Town" wedge:
> "Look, I know a full partnership is a big decision. Next time you have talent in the DC/Baltimore area, just **book a room with us for the day.** No long-term commitment. Just come in, see the space, meet the team, and let us buy you lunch. You'll see exactly why the top creators trust us."
